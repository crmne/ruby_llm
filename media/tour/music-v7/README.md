# RubyLLM tour v7: tired vs wired, re-timed

The v6 "tired vs wired" score, arranged onto the v7 picture (152.0 s, 76 bars at
120 BPM, 4/4, 28 flips). The music is v6's, unchanged: the key (C Dorian), the
home loop and its voicings (Cm9, F13, Abmaj7#11, G7b9b13), the Judge loop, the
muted-horn motif and its answer, the two worlds, the 22 presets, the three
returns and every fader, pan and send. Only the arrangement follows the new
timing.

Both cuts share one source for the music, `../music-shared/tired_wired.py`.
Each cut's `score.py` holds only its picture's timing: flips, sections,
accents, how each WIRED stretch plays, and the ending. Regenerating v6 from the
shared source reproduces its score, MIDI and controller script byte for byte,
so v6 is untouched.

## What changed relative to v6

- **Length:** 146.0 s (73 bars) became 152.0 s (76 bars). All 28 flips moved
  to v7's cues, and every one still lands on a beat with zero error.
- **Opening:** the flips are now at 0, 4, 8.5, 11.5, 14.5, 17.5, 26 and 29 s.
  Streaming (17.5 to 26 s) is a longer WIRED stretch with vibes on the
  terminal, the haiku, and the block morphing into ChatStreamJob. Open hats
  mark the whips.
- **Tools** is shorter. The vibes still climb through six steps, but on
  v7's quicker walkthrough (39.25 to 42.6 s).
- **MCP:** open hats and vibes on the GitHub cut and the Notion, Atlassian and
  Sentry whips, plus vibes on the "three lines away" title.
- **Judge (71 s) and Agent (78 s)** stay WIRED, as in v6.
- **A build into providers:** from the pull-back at 83.0 s, Agent steps up to
  level 4 (ride, brass pushes on the chord changes), with a crash and brass
  stab on that cut.
- **Providers are longer:** three flips 3.5 s apart (TIRED 88, 92, 95.5 s;
  snaps at 91.5 and 95 s). The logo wall (98.5 s) is at level 4 instead of
  v6's level 3.
- **The four pairs** (speak, paint, embed, ocr) keep v6's level 4, on the new
  flips.
- **The climax is the feature grid, 132 to 144 s**, twice v6's length. It is
  the whole band at level 5, with the horn and vibes in call and answer.
  - The collapse into labels at 140.5 s is a hit: impact, crash, a brass stab
    on the chord, a vibes run, and the kick.
  - The horn rests around the collapse and returns with the answer, whose B
    leads into the stop-time.
- **No testimonials:** v6's light bed is gone. The climax runs straight into a
  swell and the stop-time.
- **The ending** keeps v6's exact shape, now at 144 s:
  - stop-time for "You don't need Python or JavaScript for AI." (hits at
    144.0, 144.375, 144.75 and 145.25 s, then silence, then a two-16th snare
    pickup);
  - the whole band on Cm9 for "Use Ruby." at 146.0 s;
  - Cm6/9 on the end card at 147.5 s, the horn's last call leaning on D;
  - ringing to 151.875 s, then into the TIRED bar 1.

## Presets and mix

The same as v6: see the table in `../music-v6/README.md`, and
`PARTS`, `FX` and `MASTER` in `../music-shared/tired_wired.py`, which is now the
single source. WIRED is Kick, Snare, Clap, Hat, Open Hat, Ride, Shaker, Rim,
Crash, Bass (Acoustic Bass Short), Sub, Keys (Rhodes), Horn (Orchestral Brass
Muted Staccato), Brass, Vibes, Riser and Impact. TIRED is Tired Keys (Rusty
Rhodes), Tired Horn (Muted Sustained), Drone (Unstable Warm Juno Pad), Thud and
Tick, each followed by a Lo-Pass. The returns are Room Two, Hall Two and
Delay-2 3 16ths, with Solid State Glue on the master.

## Build it in Bitwig

The script is installed at
`~/Bitwig Studio/Controller Scripts/Carmine/RubyLLM Tour v7.control.js`
(`python3 music-v7/score.py --install` copies it again after a change). The v6
and v3 scripts next to it are unchanged.

1. **File > New**, for a new, empty project. The script only adds tracks and
   never touches other open projects.
2. **Settings > Controllers > Add controller > Carmine > RubyLLM Tour v7**, then
   Add. The first popup says "version 1.0 (RubyLLM tour v7, tired vs wired)
   loaded".
3. In the controller's settings, section **RubyLLM Tour v7**, press **Build**.
   Build does the following:
   - It sets 120 BPM, 4/4 and the 24 % 1/16 groove.
   - It creates the three returns and the 22 tracks with their presets, plus a
     Lo-Pass after the instrument on the five TIRED tracks.
   - It colours the tracks, then sets faders, pans and sends.
   - It puts Compressor+ on the master.
   - It writes one 76-bar launcher clip per track in scene 1.
   - It adds 37 cue markers.

   Wait for "build done". It takes several minutes while the orchestral brass
   loads.
4. Press **Lengths**. It should report "all 22 clips play 304 beats (76 bars)".
5. **Set the Lo-Pass cutoff on the five TIRED tracks** (Tired Keys, Tired Horn,
   Drone, Thud, Tick) to what you used in v6, about 400 to 600 Hz. Build loads
   the preset's default; this step is what makes each flip.
6. Press **Record**. It records scene 1 into the arranger from bar 1 and stops
   after bar 76.
7. Import `../video/v7/rubyllm-tour-v7-silent.mp4` at 1.1.1 to watch in sync.
8. Apply the same mix moves as v6: fader balance, the Rim and Crash tuning if
   you changed them, and the limiter on the master (about -14 LUFS for the
   web, true peak -1 dB).
9. Use **File > Export Audio** from 1.1.1 to 77.1.1 (152.00 s), 48 kHz,
   24-bit. Export exactly 76 bars for the loop.

If you'd rather keep your v6 project's mix, it also works to import
`midi/rubyllm-tour-v7.mid` (or the per-part files) into a copy of the v6
project. The track names and the channel per part are the same.

## Checks

```sh
python3 music-v7/score.py                           # regenerate everything
python3 music-v6/score.py                           # v6 regenerates byte for byte
node music-shared/simulate.cjs music-v7             # the script against a fake Bitwig host
SIM_REFUSE_LENGTH=1 node music-shared/simulate.cjs music-v7
python3 music-shared/verify_tw.py music-v7          # every flip against the cue MIDI, accents, harmony, stop-time, climax hit, loop
python3 music-shared/preview.py music-v7            # rough timing preview (numpy), under the video
```

These were verified by simulation and by reading the MIDI back, not by ear.
Listen before you publish.
